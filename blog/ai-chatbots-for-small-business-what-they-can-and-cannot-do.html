    AI Chatbots for Small Business: What They Can and Cannot Do

    A website chatbot can answer customers around the clock, or it can frustrate them and give wrong answers. Here is how to tell the difference before you add one to your site.

    Modern AI chatbots can hold a natural conversation, which makes them tempting for any business that gets a steady stream of questions. Done well, a chatbot answers instantly, captures leads after hours and frees your staff for work that needs a person. Done badly, it gives confident wrong answers and drives customers away.

    What a Good Small Business Chatbot Does Well

    • Answers routine questions about hours, services, service areas, parking, booking and policies, using information you have written and approved.
    • Captures inquiries after hours by collecting a name, contact details and a short description, then passing them to you.
    • Points people to the right page, such as your booking form, menu, price list or contact page.
    • Speaks several languages, which helps if your customers do not all prefer English.

    What It Should Not Do

    • Invent prices, availability or promises. If the answer is not in the information you gave it, it should say so and offer to connect the visitor with a person.
    • Give medical, legal or financial advice. Clinics, law offices and accountants need especially careful limits.
    • Hide the human option. Every chat should make it easy to call, email or request a callback.
    • Collect more personal data than it needs. Ask only for what you will actually use, and say how it will be handled.

    Questions to Ask Before You Add One

    • Where does the chatbot get its answers, and can we control and update that information ourselves?
    • What happens when it does not know something?
    • Where are conversations stored, who can read them, and for how long?
    • How much does it cost as traffic grows?
    • Can we review conversations to see what customers are really asking?

    Measure Whether It Helps

    After launch, read a sample of conversations every week at first. Look for wrong answers, questions it cannot handle and points where visitors give up. Those are your to-do list: update the information it uses, adjust its limits, or add a page to your website that answers the question properly for everyone.
